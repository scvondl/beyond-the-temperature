DATA & METHODS

Reading this atlas

NDVI

Vegetation greenness

Sentinel-2 surface reflectance, 2021–2025, on a common 20 m grid. Higher NDVI generally indicates a stronger vegetation signal. The fixed map range is −0.2 to 1.0.

NDBI

Built-surface spectral signal

Higher NDBI indicates a stronger built or bare-surface spectral signal. NDBI is not a building-area percentage and can respond to bare soil. Its fixed map range is −0.7 to 0.7.

VBS

One formula, two display ranges

100 × [0.5 × N(NDVI) + 0.5 × (1 − N(NDBI))]. The normalization reference is the original 37 supported park averages. Higher means greener with a weaker built-surface signal. Island pixels can fall outside this reference: the supplied raw VBS spans −222.78 to 169.12. About 65.6% of supported pixels fall outside 0–100.

The 0–100 view clips colours at the endpoints; the original-scale view displays −225 to 175. Both use the same underlying score. Pixel popups retain the raw VBS. No island-specific normalization has been fitted.

QA

Observation support

At least 3 clear days per qualifying quarter, 3 quarters per qualifying year, and 3 qualifying land years for the main island rasters. All 17 tiles completed. Main results support 93.76% of the planning mask after excluding dominant water. Low-support areas and water remain transparent in the index layers; the coverage layer distinguishes them.

PARKS

37 scored parks · 48 boundaries

Existing park values and eligible-year lists are copied unchanged. Park eligibility uses its original area-coverage rules. Consequently, pixel-supported island means and the original park summaries need not match exactly. Eleven parks have no supported VBS; blank values remain unavailable.

NDVI and NDBI share the NIR band; VBS combines related indicators and is not independent evidence. These results describe spectral vegetation–built characteristics. They do not measure shade, thermal comfort or user experience.
