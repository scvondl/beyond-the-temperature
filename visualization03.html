01 / ISLAND-WIDE ENVIRONMENTAL CONTEXT

Reading the landscape.

Compare the spatial distribution of vegetation (NDVI), built-up surfaces (NDBI), and the supplied VBS composite raster. These are historical environmental layers—not live weather or measured thermal comfort.

READING THE INDICATORS

NDVI describes vegetation greenness; NDBI describes built-surface spectral patterns. Neither directly measures canopy shade at a particular hour or perceived thermal comfort.

REFERENCE & COVERAGE

VBS uses the original 37-park reference scale. Raw island values can extend beyond 0–100. Missing observations remain unfilled. The review study below covers 54 listed parks, including parks without eligible mapped scores.

04 / CRITICAL INTERPRETATION

What can these data actually tell us?

01 / SPATIAL INDICATORS

Greener does not automatically mean cooler.

NDVI and NDBI characterise surfaces. Without matched local air temperature, shade and activity observations, they cannot establish how comfortable park users feel.

02 / PUBLIC REVIEWS

More mentions do not mean better experiences.

Words such as “walk”, “trail” or “family” identify themes in reviews, not the number of people doing those activities, their satisfaction or their thermal comfort.

03 / REPRESENTATION

Who is missing from the dataset?

Reviews are self-selected, unevenly sampled across parks and skewed toward English. Their time span also differs from the 2021–2025 raster period.
